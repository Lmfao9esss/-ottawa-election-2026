Overview

What Is the Ottawa Municipal Election?

A municipal election chooses local leaders, the people who manage your city's day to day services like public transportation and fixing roads. People who live in Ottawa will elect a Mayor, 24 City Councillors, and school board trustees on October 26, 2026.

🚌

Transit Crisis

OC Transpo reliability has gotten much worse. People who take OC Transpo encounter constant cancellations, slow trains and decreased frequency making it unreliable for people who use it as their main transportation method.

🏠

Housing Costs

Voters consider housing affordability as the most important issue. Several Ottawa families and young people are having to move out of the city where they grew up because of rising rents and home prices.

💰

Cost of Living

37% of Ottawa residents say cost of living is the top issue. Many households are struggling with rising taxes, inflation, and reduced city services that they depend on every day.

🛡️

Public Safety

76% of residents are concerned about the future of downtown ottawa including safety, empty storefronts, and homeless people camping there. Candidates are debating how to make Ottawa feel safe again.

The Race

Meet the Candidates

Three main candidates have declared for Ottawa's 2026 mayoral race. Each brings different experience and a different vision for the city.

🎙️

Mark Sutcliffe

Incumbent Mayor — Running for Re-Election

Age 57. Former radio host, TV personality, and business consultant. First elected mayor in 2022. Described as centrist and "middle-of-the-road." Has criticized both the provincial and federal governments for underfunding Ottawa's transit.

Key Platform Ideas
  • Cut red tape to speed up housing construction
  • Supported Lansdowne 2.0 ($418M redevelopment)
  • Focus on keeping property taxes low
  • Push higher levels of government for more transit funding
  • Promote economic development and city growth
🏘️

Jeff Leiper

City Councillor, Kitchissippi — Challenger

Born and raised in Ottawa. Councillor since 2014 (won with 85% in 2018, 72% in 2022). Chair of Ottawa's Planning and Housing Committee. Progressive candidate with deep experience in transit, housing, and neighbourhood services. Gave up his council seat to run for mayor.

Key Platform Ideas
  • Make transit frequent, reliable, and a first choice
  • Fix declining city services (sidewalks, garbage, parks)
  • Increase affordable housing supply across all wards
  • Opposed reducing LRT service frequencies
  • Voted against the 2025 city budget — more money for services
🔨

Alex Lawson

Home Builder & Business Owner — Challenger

Owns a local home framing company. Lives in Dunrobin, grew up in Heron Gate. Previously served on the Kanata–Carleton Conservative Association board. Supported Sutcliffe in 2022 but says city services have declined unacceptably. Has a team of seasoned political strategists behind him.

Key Platform Ideas
  • Fix housing, affordability, transit & public safety
  • "Hit the undo button" — restore service levels of past
  • Solution-oriented, non-partisan approach
  • Increase public safety and address downtown decline
  • Cut through gridlock — literally and politically
What's At Stake

Key Issues in Ottawa

These three issues are what Ottawa residents care about most heading into October 2026 — and the candidates have very different ideas about how to fix them.

🚌 Public Transit

Ottawa's LRT system has been plagued with breakdowns and OC Transpo has cut service frequencies to save money. This leaves many residents — especially those without cars — stranded or late. In a January 2026 poll, 11% of voters ranked transit reliability as the top issue. For daily commuters, this is a quality-of-life crisis.

Sutcliffe

Blames the province and federal government for short-changing transit funding. Wants to negotiate more money from higher levels of government rather than raising city taxes.

Leiper

Strongly opposes cuts to LRT frequency. Believes Ottawa needs to fully fund transit as a city priority so it becomes reliable and frequent — something people choose to use, not a last resort.

Lawson

Wants to restore transit to how it used to work — "let's get back to the old Ottawa where transit got you where you needed to go." Focuses on service delivery and results.

🏠 Housing Affordability

19% of voters name housing affordability as Ottawa's top problem. Rents are rising, homes cost more than ever, and many young people and families are struggling. 82% of residents say housing will influence who they vote for. This is arguably the defining issue of the 2026 race.

Sutcliffe

Has worked to cut red tape to help developers build faster. Emphasizes partnerships with builders and removing bureaucratic barriers to new housing construction.

Leiper

As Chair of the Planning and Housing Committee, his goal is to increase the availability of affordable housing, champion zoning changes, and make certain that new developments are accessible to everyone, not just those seeking high-end options.

Lawson

As an actual home builder, brings a unique industry perspective. Wants to fix the system from the ground up — reducing costs and delays that he personally sees in his business.

🛡️ Public Safety & Downtown

76% of Ottawa residents are worried about downtown — safety, empty storefronts, homelessness, and encampments. 10% of voters list homelessness as the #1 issue. The city's downtown has struggled to recover post-pandemic, and residents want action.

Sutcliffe

Backed a 5% increase to the Ottawa Police Service budget in 2026. Supports economic development to revitalize downtown and bring activity back to empty spaces.

Leiper

Also supported the police budget increase. Believes tackling homelessness requires an upstream approach — building affordable housing so fewer people are on the streets in the first place.

Lawson

Emphasizes public safety and wants to make residents feel safe downtown again. Promises real action on encampments and wants downtown to thrive economically.
